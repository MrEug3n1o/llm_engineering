# When to use the present subjunctive

The subjunctive is a mood, not a tense: it marks what is wished for, doubted, felt about or not yet real.
It almost always appears in a clause introduced by **que**, after a main clause with a different subject.

*Quiero ir.* (I want to go - one subject, infinitive.)
*Quiero que vayas.* (I want you to go - two subjects, subjunctive.)

## The WEIRDO triggers

- **W**ishes: *Espero que tengas suerte. Quiero que vengas.*
- **E**motions: *Me alegra que estés aquí. Siento que no puedas venir.*
- **I**mpersonal expressions: *Es importante que estudies. Es posible que llueva.*
- **R**ecommendations and requests: *Te recomiendo que pruebes el flan. Pido que me ayuden.*
- **D**oubt and denial: *Dudo que sea verdad. No creo que lo sepa.*
- **O**jalá (I hope / if only): *Ojalá que haga buen tiempo.* Ojalá always takes the subjunctive.

## Creer and pensar

Affirmative belief takes the indicative; negated belief takes the subjunctive:

- *Creo que es verdad.* (indicative)
- *No creo que sea verdad.* (subjunctive)

## Time clauses about the future

After **cuando**, **hasta que**, **en cuanto** and **antes de que**, use the subjunctive for events that have
not happened yet: *Te llamaré cuando llegue.* (I'll call you when I arrive.)
For habits or past events, use the indicative: *Siempre te llamo cuando llego.*

## Forming the present subjunctive

Take the **yo** form of the present indicative, drop the -o, and add the "opposite" endings:

- -ar verbs: -e, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en (hablar -> hable)
- -er and -ir verbs: -a, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an (comer -> coma, tener -> tenga)

Irregular subjunctives to memorise (**DISHES**): dar (dé), ir (vaya), ser (sea), haber (haya), estar (esté),
saber (sepa).
