# Direct and indirect object pronouns

## Direct object pronouns (what or whom the action affects)

me, te, **lo / la**, nos, os, **los / las**

*Compro el pan -> Lo compro.* *Veo a María -> La veo.*

In much of Spain, **le** is also used for a masculine person as direct object (*Le veo* for "I see him").
This is called *leísmo* and is accepted for men; in Latin America *Lo veo* is the norm.

## Indirect object pronouns (to or for whom)

me, te, **le**, nos, os, **les**

*Le doy el libro a Juan.* Spanish usually keeps the pronoun even when the person is named - this
"redundant" le is normal and expected.

## Order and the se lo rule

When both appear, the indirect comes first: *Me lo das.* (You give it to me.)

When **le** or **les** comes before lo, la, los or las, it changes to **se**:
*Le doy el libro -> Se lo doy.* (never *le lo*)
*Les mando las fotos -> Se las mando.*

Because se can mean him, her, you (usted) or them, add *a él, a ella, a usted* to clarify: *Se lo doy a ella.*

## Where the pronouns go

- Before a conjugated verb: *Lo quiero.*
- Attached to the end of an infinitive or gerund, or before the conjugated verb - both are correct:
  *Quiero comprarlo = Lo quiero comprar. Estoy leyéndolo = Lo estoy leyendo.*
- Attached to affirmative commands: *¡Dímelo!* (Tell it to me!)
- Before negative commands: *¡No me lo digas!*

Attaching pronouns often moves the stress, so a written accent is added to keep it in place: *leyendo -> leyéndolo*,
*di -> dímelo*, *compra -> cómpralo*.
