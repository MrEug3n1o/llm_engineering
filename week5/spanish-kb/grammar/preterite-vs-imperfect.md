# Preterite vs imperfect

Spanish has two simple past tenses. English often uses the same "-ed" form for both, so the choice is about how
the speaker views the action.

## The preterite (pretérito indefinido)

A completed action with a clear beginning or end, seen as a single event.

- Single completed events: *Ayer comí paella.* (Yesterday I ate paella.)
- A series of completed actions: *Me levanté, me duché y salí.*
- Actions with a defined duration: *Viví en París tres años.*
- The start or end of something: *Empezó a llover a las cinco.*

Trigger words: *ayer, anoche, el año pasado, la semana pasada, de repente, una vez, en 2010*.

Regular endings: -ar verbs take **-é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -asteis, -aron**; -er and -ir verbs take
**-í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -isteis, -ieron**.

## The imperfect (pretérito imperfecto)

Background, habit and description - the action is seen as ongoing, with no focus on its end.

- Habits in the past ("used to"): *De niño, jugaba al fútbol todos los días.*
- Descriptions and background: *Hacía sol y los pájaros cantaban.*
- Time and age in the past: *Eran las ocho. Tenía diez años.*
- Mental and physical states: *Estaba cansada. Quería dormir.*
- An action in progress when another happened (the "interrupted" action).

Trigger words: *siempre, a menudo, todos los días, normalmente, mientras, de niño, cada verano*.

Only three verbs are irregular in the imperfect: **ser** (era), **ir** (iba) and **ver** (veía).

## Using both in one sentence

The imperfect sets the scene; the preterite moves the story forward:
*Leía un libro cuando sonó el teléfono.* (I was reading a book when the phone rang.)

## Verbs that change meaning

| Verb | preterite | imperfect |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| conocer | conocí - I met (for the first time) | conocía - I knew |
| saber | supe - I found out | sabía - I knew |
| querer | quise - I tried | quería - I wanted |
| no querer | no quise - I refused | no quería - I didn't want |
| poder | pude - I managed to | podía - I was able to |
