# Gustar and verbs like it

*Me gusta el chocolate* does not literally mean "I like chocolate". It means "chocolate is pleasing to me".
The thing liked is the **subject**, and the person who likes it is the **indirect object**.

## How to build the sentence

1. Indirect object pronoun for the person: me, te, le, nos, os, les
2. **gusta** if what is liked is singular or an infinitive, **gustan** if it is plural
3. The thing liked, with its article

- *Me gusta el café.* (singular)
- *Me gustan los perros.* (plural - gustan agrees with los perros, not with me)
- *Nos gusta bailar.* (infinitive - always singular gusta, even with several infinitives: *Me gusta cantar y bailar.*)

## Clarifying and emphasising with a

Add *a + person* for emphasis or to make le/les clear:
*A ella le gusta el té. A mis padres les gustan las películas antiguas. A mí me encanta.*
Note that the pronoun le/les stays even when the person is named.

The answer to "I like it too / me neither" follows the same pattern: *A mí también. A mí tampoco.*

## Verbs that work the same way

- **encantar** - to love (things): *Me encantan las playas.*
- **interesar** - to interest: *¿Te interesa la historia?*
- **molestar** - to bother: *Le molesta el ruido.*
- **importar** - to matter: *No me importa.*
- **doler** - to hurt: *Me duele la cabeza. Me duelen los pies.*
- **faltar** - to be missing / lacking: *Nos faltan dos sillas.*
- **parecer** - to seem: *¿Qué te parece?*
- **quedar** - to have left / to fit: *Me quedan cinco euros.*

A common mistake is *Yo gusto el café* - that would mean "I am pleasing to the coffee".
