# Noun gender and articles

Every Spanish noun is masculine or feminine, and the article must agree with it in gender and number.
This is why the tutor always learns nouns together with their article: *la mesa*, never just *mesa*.

## The articles

|            | masculine | feminine |
|------------|-----------|----------|
| definite singular | el | la |
| definite plural | los | las |
| indefinite singular | un | una |
| indefinite plural | unos | unas |

Contractions: *a + el = al* (Voy al cine) and *de + el = del* (Vengo del trabajo). They never happen with the
pronoun él or with *la*.

## Patterns that usually hold

- Nouns ending in **-o** are usually masculine: *el libro, el vaso*.
- Nouns ending in **-a** are usually feminine: *la casa, la mesa*.
- **-ción, -sión, -dad, -tad, -tud, -umbre** are feminine: *la canción, la ciudad, la libertad, la costumbre*.
- **-aje** and **-or** are usually masculine: *el viaje, el garaje, el color, el amor* (but *la flor*).

## Common exceptions

- Masculine nouns ending in -a, mostly from Greek **-ma**: *el problema, el sistema, el tema, el idioma,
  el programa, el clima, el mapa*, and also *el día* and *el planeta*.
- Feminine nouns ending in -o: *la mano, la radio, la foto* (short for la fotografía), *la moto*.

## El agua: feminine nouns with a stressed a-

Feminine nouns that start with a stressed *a-* or *ha-* take **el** in the singular to avoid two "a" sounds
running together: *el agua, el águila, el hacha, el hambre, el alma, el aula*.

The noun is still feminine, so adjectives stay feminine and the plural uses las:
*el agua fría, las aguas frías*.

## Nouns that change meaning with gender

- *el capital* (money) / *la capital* (city)
- *el cometa* (comet) / *la cometa* (kite)
- *el orden* (order, arrangement) / *la orden* (a command)
- *el corte* (a cut) / *la corte* (royal court)
