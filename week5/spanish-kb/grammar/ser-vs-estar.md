# Ser vs estar

Both verbs translate as "to be". Choosing between them is about what kind of "being" you mean, not about how
permanent something is - although "permanent vs temporary" is a useful first approximation.

## When to use ser

Remember the acronym **DOCTOR**:

- **D**escription - essential characteristics: *La casa es grande.* (The house is big.)
- **O**ccupation - *Mi hermana es médica.* (My sister is a doctor.) No article before the profession.
- **C**haracteristics - personality: *Pablo es simpático.*
- **T**ime - hours, days and dates: *Son las tres. Hoy es lunes.*
- **O**rigin - *Somos de México.*
- **R**elationship - *Ana es mi prima.*

Ser is also used for where an **event** takes place: *La fiesta es en mi casa.* (The party is at my house.)

## When to use estar

Remember the acronym **PLACE**:

- **P**osition - *El libro está encima de la mesa.*
- **L**ocation of people and things: *Madrid está en España. Estoy en la oficina.*
- **A**ction - progressive tenses with the gerund: *Estoy comiendo.* (I am eating.)
- **C**ondition - *La sopa está fría. Estoy cansado.*
- **E**motion - *Están contentos.*

Death is a classic trap: *Mi abuelo está muerto* uses estar, even though nothing could be more permanent.

## Adjectives that change meaning

| Adjective | with ser | with estar |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| aburrido | boring | bored |
| listo | clever | ready |
| malo | bad (a bad person) | ill |
| rico | rich | tasty |
| verde | green | unripe |
| orgulloso | arrogant | proud (of someone) |

*Juan es aburrido* means John is a boring person; *Juan está aburrido* means John is bored right now.

## Conjugation reminder

- ser (present): soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son
- estar (present): estoy, estás, está, estamos, estáis, están
