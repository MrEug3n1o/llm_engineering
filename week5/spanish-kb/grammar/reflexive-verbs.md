# Reflexive verbs

A reflexive verb has an action that falls back on the subject. In the dictionary it ends in **-se**:
*levantarse* (to get up), *ducharse* (to shower), *llamarse* (to be called).

## Reflexive pronouns

| subject | pronoun | levantarse |
|---------|---------|------------|
| yo | me | me levanto |
| tú | te | te levantas |
| él / ella / usted | se | se levanta |
| nosotros | nos | nos levantamos |
| vosotros | os | os levantáis |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | se | se levantan |

The pronoun goes before a conjugated verb, or attached to an infinitive, gerund or affirmative command:
*Me voy a duchar = Voy a ducharme. ¡Levántate!*

## A daily routine

*Me despierto a las siete, me levanto, me ducho, me visto y me peino.*
(I wake up at seven, get up, shower, get dressed and comb my hair.)

With body parts and clothes, Spanish uses the article, not a possessive: *Me lavo las manos* (I wash my hands),
*Se pone la chaqueta* (She puts on her jacket).

## Verbs whose meaning changes with -se

- *ir* (to go) / *irse* (to leave): *Me voy.*
- *dormir* (to sleep) / *dormirse* (to fall asleep)
- *llamar* (to call) / *llamarse* (to be called): *Me llamo Ana.*
- *poner* (to put) / *ponerse* (to put on; to become): *Se puso rojo.*
- *acordar* (to agree) / *acordarse de* (to remember)
- *quedar* (to arrange to meet) / *quedarse* (to stay)

## Reciprocal se

In the plural the same pronouns can mean "each other": *Nos queremos.* (We love each other.)
*Se escriben cada semana.* (They write to each other every week.)
