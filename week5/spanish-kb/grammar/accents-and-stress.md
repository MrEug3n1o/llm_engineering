# Written accents and word stress

Spanish spelling tells you exactly which syllable to stress. The written accent (*la tilde*) only appears when a
word breaks the two default rules.

## The default rules

1. Words ending in a **vowel, n or s** are stressed on the **second-to-last** syllable: *ca-sa, ha-blan, li-bros*.
2. Words ending in any **other consonant** are stressed on the **last** syllable: *ha-blar, ciu-dad, re-loj*.

## When to write the accent

Write the tilde when the stress breaks those rules:

- *ca-fé, can-ción, in-glés* - end in a vowel, n or s but are stressed on the last syllable.
- *ár-bol, lá-piz, fá-cil* - end in another consonant but are stressed on the second-to-last.
- Every word stressed on the third-to-last syllable (*esdrújulas*) takes an accent: *mú-si-ca, te-lé-fo-no,
  rá-pi-do, sá-ba-do*.

Plurals can gain or lose an accent to keep the stress in place: *joven -> jóvenes*, *canción -> canciones*.

## The diacritic accent: same spelling, different word

Some short words carry an accent only to tell them apart from a twin:

| with accent | without accent |
|-------------|----------------|
| tú - you | tu - your |
| él - he | el - the |
| mí - me | mi - my |
| sí - yes | si - if |
| sé - I know / be! | se - reflexive pronoun |
| té - tea | te - you (object pronoun) |
| más - more | mas - but (literary) |

## Question words

Question and exclamation words carry an accent, including in indirect questions:
*¿Qué?, ¿Cómo?, ¿Dónde?, ¿Cuándo?, ¿Quién?, ¿Cuánto?* - *No sé dónde vive.*
Without the accent they are connectors: *Vivo donde trabajo. Creo que sí.*

## Breaking up a diphthong

An accent on i or u splits it from a neighbouring vowel into its own syllable: *dí-a, pa-ís, ba-úl, Ma-rí-a*.
